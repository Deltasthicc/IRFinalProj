## 2. Abstract

Analysts who ask questions about company filings need exact figures and the passage that supports them. We present TradeMesh, search and question answering over the latest Form 10-K filings of 20 US companies (11,964 passages). A classic IR core written from the lecture formulas (positional index, TF-IDF, BM25, spelling-corrected company names) is combined with dense retrieval, rank fusion, cross-encoder reranking, a company mention graph, and answers that cite their passages, are checked by a verifier and abstain when evidence is thin. On 20 test needs, with hypotheses fixed beforehand and paired bootstrap intervals, hybrid retrieval with reranking is strongest (nDCG@10 0.828 against 0.699 for BM25, difference +0.129, 95% CI +0.052 to +0.213), but hybrid retrieval is tied with dense retrieval alone and the reranker's gain over hybrid is inconclusive. Spelling correction resolved 39 of 40 misspelt names. In an answer study rated blind by two humans (mean of the two raters), retrieval with reranking scores 1.20 of 2 against 0.575 for closed-book (+0.625, CI +0.350 to +0.900), while BM25-based RAG (0.90) is only weakly better than closed-book (+0.325, CI +0.050 to +0.575, carried by one of the two raters). The model's own insufficient-evidence verdict separated 6 of 6 unanswerable test questions from 20 answerable ones; a retrieval-score threshold did not. Limits: small collections, model-assisted relevance judgments, two human raters who are both on the team, one small generator, and a page-image track that was scoped out.

**Keywords:** information retrieval, BM25, dense retrieval, reciprocal rank fusion, retrieval-augmented generation, financial filings, verification.
